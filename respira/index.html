Michèle has spent two decades studying how the body heals — first as a scientist, then on the mat, and finally through the breath.

She holds an MPH and a PhD in Infectious Diseases and Immunology from UC Berkeley. She found Ashtanga Vinyasa in a San Francisco studio in 2003, looking for a way through the stress of doctoral work, and never really left. In 2004 she moved to Florence and opened It's Yoga Firenze — now INBODHI Yoga — where she has taught ever since.

respira is her practice: the physiology of the nervous system alongside Vedic chant, conscious breathing and ceremony. What she teaches, she has lived.

her story

Florence, 2003 — today

portrait · 4:5

In August 2015, a car accident nearly took Michèle's life. It took her left leg.

What carried her through was breath. In the hospital, and then through eight months bed-bound, breathing was the one practice still available to her — and it turned out to be enough to begin with.

During those months she began studying Vedic chanting with Menaka Desikachar through the Krishnamacharya Healing and Yoga Foundation, learning a tradition that uses sound and breath as medicine. A year after the accident she found Transformational Breath®, and with it the emotional release that physical recovery alone could not reach.

It's not what happens to you, but what you do with it that is important.

She has since trained as a Certified Transformational Breath® Facilitator and Group Leader, a Certified Vedic Chant Teacher, and has studied healing practices with Amazonian tribes and in the Paqo Andino lineage of the Andes.

She teaches from all of it. Not as theory — as the route she actually walked.

transformational breath®

The breath you already
know how to take

inhaleexhale

A full, conscious, connected breath — no pause between the inhale and the exhale — held for long enough that the body starts to let go of what it has been carrying.

Sessions are guided from beginning to end. You lie down, you breathe, and Michèle works with the pattern that shows up in your body. Most people leave lighter than they arrived, and a little surprised by it.

Despacho Ceremony

An offering and prayer, an act of love, that reminds us of our deep connection with all beings, elements, spirits and sacred places.

The ceremony creates harmony — an opportunity to enter into the essential unity of all things. Held for groups marking a beginning, an ending, or a moment that deserves more than words.

Cacao Ceremony

Ceremonial cacao, shared slowly and with intention, alongside breath, chant and stillness. A gentle opening — the kind of gathering where a group arrives as strangers and leaves as something else.

Offered in Tuscany and on retreat, for private groups and open circles.
